How to interpret this comparison

Resolution markers are nominal reference beams, not a projected beam simulation. Each band uses its adopted central frequency. A finer beam alone does not guarantee detection of expanding ejecta.

The visibility test requires at least one continuous hour above the elevation limit. For the VLBA, EVN, EAVN and Australian LBA, at least three stations must share the same interval. The subset maximises station count, then physical baseline extent. Subset beams are scaled by the ratio of maximum physical baselines, not a calculated point-spread function. Station availability by band is not modelled. Other arrays use a representative site; LOFAR International uses the Dutch core and needs a station-level check.

Image quality is only an antenna-count proxy, N². It does not describe baseline distribution or calibration. Sensitivity is one representative reference per facility, often at a different frequency and weighting from the finest beam. Faded bars indicate provisional values. Noise scales as 1/√time as a thermal-equivalent comparison; confusion and calibration floors are not included. Subset sensitivity is left unestimated without station SEFDs.

The time axis is angular scale divided by proper motion. Distance is unnecessary for this conversion. No jet dynamics or ejecta evolution is calculated. Visibility is repeating sidereal geometry, not a dated observing schedule or guarantee of telescope time.
